\begin{abstract}
A classifier is $\epsilon$-robust if it assigns the same label to any two inputs within distance $\epsilon$.
On connected domains such as $\mathbb{R}^n$, every $\epsilon$-robust classifier is constant.
Existing relaxations therefore weaken the guarantee to hand-picked or high-confidence inputs, or impose a bound on the probability of violations.
Probabilistic bounds, however, constrain the probability of robustness violations, not where they lie.
To bridge this gap, we introduce $(\mu,\epsilon)$-robustness, which requires every input to lie within the distance $\mu$ of a point with the same label at which the classifier is locally $\epsilon$-robust. It coincides with $\epsilon$-robustness at $\mu=0$.
We study $(\mu,\epsilon)$-robustness over semimetric spaces, which capture non-metric similarity notions used in fairness and natural language processing. 
Since deployed classifiers operate on finitely many representable inputs, we pay particular attention to finite domains such as IEEE 754 floating-point numbers.
We characterize $(\mu,\epsilon)$-robustness by a covering condition that extends the classical equivalence between $\epsilon$-robustness and separability, and we give necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of a non-constant robust classifier.
We derive bounds on $\epsilon$ and $\mu$, in terms of the spacing between consecutive representable values, beyond which every $(\mu, \epsilon)$-robust classifier is constant; 
they hold on totally ordered domains and on partially ordered ones admitting a suitable decomposition.
All results are mechanized in Lean~4: machine-checked proofs were developed with AI assistance; formal statements were validated against the paper by hand.

\end{abstract}
